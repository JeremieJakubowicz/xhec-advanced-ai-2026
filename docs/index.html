      Inside the networks: how the prediction is computed

      fifteen short steps through the two architectures: embedding, convolution window, gate, residual stream, receptive field, logits; then the recurrent cell, its three gates, its memory, the steps, layer 2, the reach of the memory, the prediction.

      Everything above comes out of vectors of a few hundred numbers, transformed step by step. This section follows the computation over the last sixteen tokens up to the selected one: one column per token, one flat strip per vector. A strip is not a matrix: it is one vector of 256 numbers (512 for the LSTM's memory and output) laid side by side, one thin vertical stripe per coordinate, colour for the value; its height means nothing. The only matrices here are the weights, which sit on the edges. Press play, or step through it: the CNN slides a window of three positions along the text, one layer after the other; the LSTM applies one and the same cell at every token, handing its memory on to the next step. Click any cell to see the actual numbers it was computed from.

      drawing convention: a vector is a flat strip, one row of cells (256 or 512 numbers); a matrix is a block, a grid of cells, shown as a thumbnail (up to 8,000 × 256); sizes are not to scale. On the diagrams a small grid marks the place where a matrix multiplies a vector.

          2a. The CNN: a window of three that slides along the text

          Each layer reads the stream at three consecutive positions (t−2, t−1, t) and writes a new value at position t, added to the old one. The three edges into a cell are the layer's three weight matrices, W₂⁽ℓ⁾, W₁⁽ℓ⁾, W₀⁽ℓ⁾, one per offset and the same at every position; at the start of the text, only the edges that have something to read exist. Each matrix has 512 rows, so their sum with the bias is one vector a of 512 numbers, read as two halves, a = (u ; g): the first 256 are the content u, the last 256 the gate g, which goes through a sigmoid before multiplying u. A layer is not one filter but 512: row i of the three matrices is filter i, a stencil of 3 × 256 weights that slides along the text and produces one number per position (the first 256 filters give u, the last 256 give g); the matrices are just these stencils sorted by offset. The panel below the diagram can zoom on any one filter and show its stencil. After four layers a position has seen nine tokens, and nothing beyond. No position waits for another: a layer can be computed at all positions at once, which is why the CNN is fast. When the window starts later in the text, the two dimmed columns on the left are the two tokens before it. While stepping: the dashed bracket is the window being read, the solid frame the cell being written, and the light orange cells are all those that have contributed to it, layer after layer.

          2b. The LSTM: one cell, applied at every token, passing its memory along

          The same weights are applied at every step. At token t, each layer receives the input of the moment (the embedding for layer 1, the output of layer 1 for layer 2) together with its own previous output h and memory c; from them it computes three gates (f: how much of the memory to keep, i: how much of the candidate to write, o: how much of the memory to show), updates the memory and emits the output. Position t cannot start before position t−1 is finished: the steps are sequential. Anything from far back can reach the prediction, but only by surviving every forget gate in between; the last row shows how much of the memory as it was after each step is still present at the selected token.

          inside one box: the cell of layer 2 at the current step, wired as in Christopher Olah's Understanding LSTM Networks

          The small grids on the four wires are the weight matrices, Wv on xt and Uv on ht−1, one pair per gate. The memory line runs along the top, from ct−1 to ct: the forget gate multiplies it, the input gate lets a share of the candidate in, and the sum is the new memory, handed unchanged to the next step. The output gate reads the memory through a tanh, and what comes out is ht: it goes to the next step and up to the next layer. The three gates and the candidate are all computed from the same two vectors, xt and ht−1, each with its own weights. The gates are coloured by their mean over the 512 slots at the current step: a forget gate near 1 keeps the memory, near 0 wipes it.
